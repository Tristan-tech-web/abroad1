### Understanding the Indonesian vocational system

Indonesian upper-secondary education splits into two tracks. **SMA** is the general
academic track. **SMK** is the vocational track: a three-year programme combining the
national core curriculum with an occupational specialisation and a mandatory industrial
placement. Roughly [GAP: persen, kalau ada datanya] of Indonesian upper-secondary
students attend SMK.

Two consequences matter when reading an SMK transcript:

**The curriculum is fixed, not elected.** Students do not choose courses. There is no
honours track, no Advanced Placement, no International Baccalaureate, and no
Cambridge programme available anywhere in the Indonesian SMK system. A student cannot
demonstrate ambition through course selection, because no selection exists. Where a
student here has pursued advanced material, they have done so outside the timetable.

**The stated purpose is employment, not university.** National vocational policy targets
direct entry into industry. Our own institutional goal is 90% of graduates entering
employment, higher education, or entrepreneurship. This is the system working as
designed, not a school underperforming.

### Programmes of study

Four three-year specialisations:

| Code | Programme | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **PPLG** | Software and Game Development | Web, mobile, game development; databases; software engineering; introductory AI and machine learning |
| **TJKT** | Computer Network and Telecommunications Engineering | Networking, infrastructure, telecommunications |
| **DKV** | Visual Communication Design | Graphic design, multimedia |
| **BD** | Digital Business | E-commerce, digital marketing |

All students additionally take the Indonesian national core: Religious Education,
Pancasila and Civics, Indonesian Language, **English**, Mathematics, Natural and Social
Sciences (IPAS), History, Physical Education, Arts and Culture, and Entrepreneurship
(PKWU). Balinese Language and Japanese are offered as regional and foreign language
subjects.

**Performance-based graduation requirements.** Two elements have no direct equivalent
in a US transcript and should be read as substantive academic work:

- **PKL (Praktik Kerja Lapangan)** — a compulsory industrial placement of up to six
  months in Grade 12, served at a partner company, assessed by both the employer and
  the school. Students work on production problems, not observation tasks.
- **UKK (Uji Kompetensi Keahlian)** — a competency examination administered at the end
  of the programme and assessed against national occupational standards, in part by
  external industry assessors rather than by school staff.

The school maintains over 50 industry partnerships, including AXIOO, Telkom Akses,
PLN Icon Plus, and Bank Fajar.

### Testing

Indonesia has no national standardised examination equivalent to the SAT or ACT, and
no such test is required for Indonesian university admission from the SMK track.
Neither the SAT nor the ACT is taught, prepared for, or administered at this school,
and no student here has previously sat either.

Students who take these tests do so entirely at their own expense and initiative, travel
to a commercial test centre outside the school, and prepare without institutional support.
The same applies to IELTS and the Duolingo English Test. Where a student from this school
submits such a score, it was obtained under those conditions.

## Catatan untuk Tristan

Bagian yang paling banyak bekerja untukmu adalah **"Understanding the Indonesian
vocational system"** dan **"Testing"**. Dua bagian itu yang mengubah "SAT 1020" dari
angka lemah menjadi angka yang diperoleh tanpa satupun dukungan institusional.

Jangan biarkan sekolah memotong dua bagian itu karena terdengar merendahkan sekolah.
Itu bukan merendahkan, itu konteks, dan NACAC secara eksplisit menganjurkan sekolah
non-Amerika menjelaskan kesulitan tambahan yang dihadapi siswanya dalam tes standar.
