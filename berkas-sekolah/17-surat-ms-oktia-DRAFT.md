## Draf surat

To the Admissions Committee,

I teach English at SMK TI Bali Global Badung. English is a required subject here, but it
is only a subject: our language of instruction is Indonesian, and no other class is taught
in English. That is the context in which I have watched Tristan.

He stood out to me early, and not for the reason I expected. His grammar is good but it is
not the best in the school. What is different is what he does with the language.

Most of my students learn English as something to be completed. They produce it for me,
on my terms, for a mark. Tristan uses it to get things. When he cannot find an answer in
Indonesian, he goes looking in English, and when he cannot find it in English either, he
writes to someone who might know.

This year he did exactly that. He reached a question in his independent study that he could
not resolve from anything available to him, and he wrote to a professor of physics at
Claremont in California to ask about it. He was sixteen years old at the time [GAP: mohon
konfirmasi usianya saat itu — Juli 2026]. He had never met her. She answered him, and they
have now exchanged more than thirty messages about his work.

I have read some of that correspondence [GAP: mohon benar-benar membacanya sebelum menulis
kalimat ini]. I want to describe what I saw in it as an English teacher, because it is
not what I could have assessed in my classroom.

He writes to be understood by someone who owes him nothing. His sentences are short when
the content is difficult. He states what he is confident about and separates it from what
he is not, in plain language, without hedging into vagueness. When he is reporting that
something he hoped for did not work, he says so directly in the first paragraph rather
than burying it. When the professor told him his abstract was impenetrable, his reply
began by agreeing with her.

I have taught English for [GAP: berapa tahun]. I do not often see a student who can
concede a point cleanly in a second language. It requires being secure enough not to
defend yourself, and having enough control of the language to be brief.

In class, he is the student who asks the question the others were not going to ask. He is
not the loudest and he does not perform. He waits, and then he asks something that changes
the direction of the lesson. Sometimes this is inconvenient for me, because the answer is
not in my lesson plan. [GAP: satu momen konkret di kelas Ibu akan sangat membantu di sini
— apa yang dia tanyakan, dan apa yang terjadi setelahnya.]

I should be honest about his written work for my subject. Our curriculum does not ask
students to write extended argumentative essays; the vocational syllabus covers functional
and practical English. So the committee will not find much formal writing from him on his
transcript, and that is a limitation of what I have been able to teach him rather than a
reflection of what he can do. His strongest writing has been produced outside my classroom,
for an audience harder to satisfy than I am.

What I can say about his character is this. He is the sort of student who tells you when he
does not understand. In my experience that is rarer than confidence and worth much more.
He does not pretend, and when he is corrected he does not become defensive; he becomes
faster.

I recommend him warmly and without reservation.

[GAP: nama, jabatan, tanda tangan, tanggal]
