On 15 Sep 2026, after a bad night and a caffeine crash, the line that changed his state was
"ini rencana, bukan ujian, tidak ada bagian di sini yang bisa kamu gagalkan." He said it calmed him
far more than usual.

**Why:** nearly everything in his next nine months is scoreable. SAT has a number, Duolingo has a
threshold, applications have accept or reject, the paper has peer review, the calendar has done and
not-done. Self-imposed standards are one of the four pressures he named. So a task with no pass mark
is rare for him, and naming it as such removes a load he carries by default.

**How to apply:** separate the two kinds of task explicitly. Things with a real number — SAT 1170,
Duolingo 135, deadlines — keep their number, and never soften those; he has asked not to have
standards lowered. Everything else (a nap, a walk, reading rule boxes, attending a mentor-led
session, a first draft) has no threshold, so say so out loud. Only use the phrasing where it is
literally true; it worked because it was accurate, not because it was soothing. Saying it about a
scored item would read as false comfort and cost trust.

**Corollary, added 15 Sep 2026.** When he sets a workload, he sets it as a floor and says he will
tell me when to add. Never raise it unilaterally. A floor he can always clear stays a floor; a floor
quietly raised becomes a test, which destroys the same property. Wait for him to ask, then add.
