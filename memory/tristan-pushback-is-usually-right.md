When Tristan pushes back on a recommendation, treat it as a signal to re-check the underlying
evidence, not as a request for reassurance. On 12 Sep 2026 he challenged three separate
recommendations in one session and was right all three times:

- **TOEFL for Princeton.** He asked why not just sit IELTS twice, since that is what he has 29
  mentor sessions for. Re-checking found Princeton's own page lists Duolingo among four accepted
  tests; the source behind the TOEFL line was a 2022 article that was *asking* Princeton to accept
  it. Removing TOEFL cut Rp 3.44jt from the budget.
- **The loan axis.** He noticed "no-loan" was mentioned for one college and asked why it was not a
  field on every college. It should have been: international students cannot get US federal loans
  and private ones need a US cosigner, so a loan line in a package that "meets full need" is an
  unfillable gap, not a debt. That makes it a harder filter for him than need-blind status.
- **Timing the ask to Sarah Marzen.** He objected to loading a dated request on someone who had
  twice said she was busy. He was right on manners, and the deeper error was mine: the ITW deadline
  was never hers to carry, since the paper is single-author and he can submit alone.

**Why:** his instincts on strategy, social judgement, and where a claim is load-bearing are strong,
and he reads sources carefully. Defending a position costs him accuracy on decisions that involve
real money and one-shot deadlines.

**How to apply:** when he questions something, go back to primary sources before answering. Say
plainly what was wrong and what changed, fix the artifacts, and move on without over-apologising.
